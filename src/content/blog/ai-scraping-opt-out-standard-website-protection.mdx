**OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, and Anthropic** announced on **October 30, 2026** that they will all honor a single, shared **robots.txt token** — reported as `__usage_ai-training` — letting any website opt its content out of AI model training across all four companies' crawlers with one line, instead of configuring separate rules for each. It's a genuinely useful simplification for any business that publishes content online. It's also narrower than the headlines suggest, and the rollout timeline means it does essentially nothing for most sites today.

## What this actually covers — and what it doesn't

The single biggest misunderstanding likely to spread about this announcement is treating it as a general "block AI from my site" switch. It isn't:

- **It only addresses AI training use**, not whether your content is crawled, indexed, or surfaced in AI-generated answers. Microsoft explicitly separates "AI training" from "AI grounding" (the use of content to generate an AI answer in real time) as two different opt-outs, and confirms sites remain visible in Bing's search results either way.
- **Google has separately confirmed its own opt-out doesn't stop content from being used in Google's AI Overviews** — the AI-generated summary boxes that increasingly sit above standard search results. Opting out of training and opting out of being summarized are not the same lever.
- **The rollout timeline is uneven and, for most companies, not yet active.** Anthropic says it has started implementing the token already. OpenAI gives no firm date beyond "coming months." Microsoft has committed to February 1, 2027. Google has only said "sometime in 2027." A business that updates its robots.txt today and assumes it's immediately protected across all four companies is relying on a commitment, not a working mechanism, for three of the four.

## Why this matters for a business website, beyond the AI-ethics debate

Whatever your position on AI training generally, there's a concrete business reason to understand this: content your business has invested in — service pages, case studies, original research, blog posts like this one — has real value as training data, and until now, the only way to protect it required separately configuring and maintaining rules for each AI company's specific crawler, a task most small businesses never got around to doing comprehensively. A single shared signal is a meaningfully lower-effort way to make an intentional choice here, once it's actually live.

## What to do now

- **Decide your actual position before implementing anything** — opting out of AI training isn't automatically the right call for every business; some genuinely want their expertise surfaced by AI assistants as a discovery channel, which is a different question from training-data consent.
- **If you do want to opt out, add the shared token now, but don't treat it as fully effective yet** — per Anthropic's own earlier implementation, it provides real protection against their crawler today; for OpenAI, Microsoft, and Google, continue maintaining your existing crawler-specific blocks until each company's own rollout date passes.
- **Separate "training opt-out" from "AI-answer visibility" in your own thinking** — if your concern is really about your content appearing (uncredited) inside AI-generated search summaries rather than training data specifically, this token doesn't address that concern at all; that's a different, still largely unresolved question.
- **Revisit this in February and again later in 2027** as Microsoft's and Google's committed dates arrive, to confirm the mechanism is actually functioning as described rather than assuming a 2026 announcement means 2026 enforcement.

## AI-scraping opt-out decision checklist

- [ ] Decide your business's actual position on AI training use of your published content
- [ ] If opting out, add the shared `__usage_ai-training` robots.txt directive now
- [ ] Keep existing crawler-specific blocks in place for companies that haven't yet activated the shared token (OpenAI, Microsoft, Google as of this writing)
- [ ] Separately evaluate your position on AI-answer/grounding visibility, which this token does not cover
- [ ] Set a reminder to verify enforcement after Microsoft's (February 2027) and Google's (2027) committed rollout dates

## Key takeaways

The shared AI-training opt-out token is a real, useful simplification — but it's a training-data signal honored on four different timelines, not an immediate, comprehensive "keep AI away from my content" switch. Add it to your robots.txt if opting out matches your business's actual position, understand clearly what it doesn't cover, and don't let the announcement's broad framing substitute for checking whether each company has actually started honoring it yet.
