Gap analysis and hands-on help getting ready for the standards your clients, your regulator, or your own risk appetite actually ask about — including the frameworks increasingly driving compliance budgets across Saudi Arabia and the wider GCC.

## NCA ECC compliance consulting for Saudi Arabia

Saudi Arabia's National Cybersecurity Authority (NCA) publishes the Essential Cybersecurity Controls (ECC-2:2024) — the baseline control set originally built for government, critical-national-infrastructure and sensitive-data organisations operating in the Kingdom. What's changed the demand picture is the NCA's follow-on National Cybersecurity Controls for Non-Critical Information Assets (NCNICC-1:2025), which pulled a meaningfully wider set of private-sector companies into formal scope for the first time — meaning a lot of Saudi businesses that reasonably assumed cybersecurity regulation applied to "someone else" now need an honest gap assessment.

ECC-2:2024 (the current revision, restructured from the earlier ECC-1:2018) is organised into four main domains — cybersecurity governance, cybersecurity defence, cybersecurity resilience, and third-party & cloud computing cybersecurity — each broken into subdomains and specific controls. Industrial control systems content was split out of ECC-2 into the NCA's separate OTCC framework, so an ICS-heavy operator may need both. A gap analysis against ECC-2:2024 looks at where your current controls, policies and evidence stand against each applicable domain, not just whether you have an ISO certificate to point at (ISO 27001 overlaps substantially with the ECC's intent but doesn't automatically satisfy it control-for-control).

Separately, the NCA's newer National Cybersecurity Controls for Non-Critical Information Assets (NCNICC-1:2025, adopted December 2025) set a baseline control set for private-sector entities outside critical national infrastructure — the development that's pulled a much wider range of ordinary Saudi businesses into scope for formal NCA-aligned cybersecurity obligations, often for the first time. No public transition deadline has been published for it as of this writing, but the practical, sensible posture is to move toward demonstrable compliance now rather than wait for an enforcement date.

If you're unsure whether your business is actually in scope for ECC, NCNICC, or neither, that's the right first question to ask in a free consultation, before committing to a full gap analysis — scoping wrong wastes budget on the wrong baseline.

## SAMA Cybersecurity Framework readiness

Saudi banks, insurers, finance companies and SAMA-licensed fintechs work against a separate, sector-specific framework: the Saudi Central Bank's (SAMA) Cybersecurity Framework, which expects regulated entities to reach and evidence a defined cybersecurity maturity level across its control domains, not just implement controls in isolation. For financial-sector businesses, this is usually the primary framework to scope against, with NCA ECC obligations layered on top depending on classification — see our [cybersecurity compliance guide for GCC businesses](/blog/gcc-cybersecurity-compliance-sama-nesa/) for how the regional frameworks relate to each other, and how they compare to the UAE's NESA-derived requirements.
