Gartner's latest forecast puts worldwide information security end-user spending at roughly **$247.5 billion in 2026, rising to $276.1 billion in 2027** — and the business-only portion (excluding consumer security spending) at **$238.4 billion rising to $266.8 billion** over the same period. Those numbers are useful less as a target to match than as a signal: security spending is still growing faster than general IT budgets industry-wide, which matters when a business is deciding whether 2027 is the year to hold its own security budget flat.

## Where the growth is actually concentrated

Not evenly. Gartner separately forecasts the market specifically for **securing AI systems** to reach **$4.8 billion in 2027**, a reported **68.7% jump** — by far the fastest-growing slice of the overall figure. That's a meaningful data point for any business that has adopted AI tools (Copilot, AI coding assistants, customer-facing chatbots) without a corresponding look at how those tools are secured or governed, which describes a large share of SMEs right now. Our [shadow AI](/blog/shadow-ai-security-risks/) and [AI application security checklist](/blog/ai-application-security-checklist/) articles cover the specific gaps this spending category is responding to.

## The real affordability problem

Industry commentary — Dark Reading has specifically framed this as a looming **"affordability crisis"** — points at the structural issue underneath the aggregate growth number: smaller businesses don't have the deep reserves a large enterprise can absorb a single bad year with. A breach that's an uncomfortable line item for a multinational can be existential for a company with a handful of employees. That asymmetry is the actual reason an SME's security budget conversation should look different from simply scaling down an enterprise's percentages — the risk per dollar of revenue is not the same, and the budget shouldn't pretend it is.

## A 2027 planning survey found real optimism — worth reading carefully

A Gartner-linked 2027 planning survey of security decision-makers found **16% expecting a budget increase above 10%**, and **44% expecting an increase in the 5–10% range** — meaning a clear majority expect growth, not cuts. Read that as a planning signal, not a formula: those figures skew toward organizations with existing, maturing security programs making incremental additions. A business still missing fundamentals (no MFA everywhere, no tested backup, no documented incident response plan) is better served by a larger one-time correction now, followed by steadier increases after — not by applying an industry-average percentage to a budget that was already too thin.

## A practical budget framework for a business your size

Build the budget in this order, not from a vendor's product list:

1. **Identity fundamentals first** — MFA on every account that supports it, a password manager, and a move toward passkeys where available. Cheapest category on this list, and it closes more realistic risk than almost anything that follows.
2. **Backup and recovery, tested — not just scheduled.** A backup that's never been tested for actual restoration is a false sense of security, not a control.
3. **A written, basic incident response plan.** Doesn't need to be elaborate; it needs to exist and name who does what in the first hour of a bad day.
4. **Vendor and third-party visibility.** Know which of your critical tools and suppliers represent concentration risk (see our [vendor risk guide](/blog/third-party-vendor-risk-management-smb/)), since a vendor's failure becomes your outage regardless of your own budget.
5. **Compliance requirements your actual clients ask for** — not a generic certification chase, but the specific frameworks (ISO 27001, client security questionnaires, regional requirements) your real or prospective clients are actually asking about.
6. **AI tool governance**, given this is the fastest-growing spending category industry-wide and the one most SMEs have least visibility into right now.
7. **Only after the above: broader monitoring, a penetration test cadence, or a security awareness training program**, sized to what the business can realistically sustain year over year rather than treated as a one-off box to check.

## 2027 budget planning checklist

- [ ] MFA and a password manager confirmed in place across every account that supports them
- [ ] Backup recovery actually tested in the last 12 months, not just scheduled
- [ ] A written incident response plan exists and names specific people for the first hour of a response
- [ ] Critical vendors and suppliers reviewed for single-provider concentration risk
- [ ] Compliance priorities set by what real clients actually ask for, not a generic checklist
- [ ] AI tools in active use across the business have been identified and reviewed, not assumed to be already covered
- [ ] Monitoring, penetration testing, and awareness training budgeted last, sized to what's sustainable year over year

## Key takeaways

Gartner's 2027 numbers say the direction of travel industry-wide is more security spending, not less — useful context, but not a budget plan on its own. The more useful discipline for a smaller business is building next year's number from a short list of actual, documented risks rather than a vendor's catalog or an industry-average percentage, with identity fundamentals and a tested backup ahead of anything more advanced. The fastest-growing line item globally — securing AI tools — is also the one most SMEs have given the least thought to so far, which makes it worth a specific look before the rest of the budget gets allocated.
