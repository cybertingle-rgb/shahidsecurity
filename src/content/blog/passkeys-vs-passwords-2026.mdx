Passwordless authentication has been "coming soon" for most of the past decade. In 2026, the FIDO Alliance's State of Passkeys report put a number on how far that's actually progressed: roughly 5 billion passkeys now in active use, consumer awareness around 90%, and most enterprises either deploying or actively piloting passkeys for employee sign-in. The technology stopped being a future bet and became a practical option most businesses can roll out today.

## What a passkey actually is

A passkey is a cryptographic key pair — a private key that stays securely on your device (phone, laptop, security key) and never leaves it, and a public key registered with the website or service you're signing into. When you log in, your device proves it holds the private key through a cryptographic challenge; nothing resembling a shared secret ever gets typed, transmitted, or stored on the service's end the way a password does.

That structural difference is what eliminates the two biggest password weaknesses at once:

- **Phishing resistance.** There's no secret for a user to be tricked into typing into a fake login page, because authentication never involves typing anything — a passkey simply won't respond to a site it wasn't registered with, regardless of how convincing a phishing page looks.
- **No credential reuse risk.** Each passkey is specific to one service. A password-reuse-driven breach at one company can't cascade into another company's accounts the way password reuse does, because there's no shared secret to reuse in the first place.

## The adoption numbers, as of 2026

The FIDO Alliance's 2026 survey (roughly 11,000 consumers and 1,400 enterprise decision-makers across ten countries) found:

- **~5 billion passkeys** now in active use globally.
- **~90% consumer awareness**, up sharply from the prior year, with **75% of consumers** having enabled a passkey on at least one account.
- Regular, ongoing use is lower than one-time setup — around **49% of consumers** report using passkeys regularly, which reflects the real adoption curve: awareness and initial setup move faster than habitual use.

On the enterprise side, figures vary by survey but point the same direction: one 2026 source put enterprise passkey deployment (deployed, piloting, or rolling out) at 68% of organizations for employee sign-in; a separate 2025 HID/FIDO Alliance survey found 87% of enterprises actively deploying or piloting FIDO2 passkeys. The gap between surveys reflects different samples and definitions, but both numbers describe genuine majority momentum, not an early experiment.

Reported usability data is also notable for a security control, since passkeys are a rare case where the more secure option is also measurably faster: login success rates run around 93% for passkeys versus 63% for passwords, with average sign-in time dropping to roughly 8.5 seconds versus over 30 seconds for password-based logins that often involve a reset or retry.

## Why this matters for business email compromise specifically

Shahid Security has covered how 2026-era business email compromise attacks increasingly bypass MFA — not by breaking the cryptography, but by tricking users into approving a push notification or completing a phishing flow that captures a session token after a password and MFA code are both entered. Passkeys close that specific gap, because there's no password to phish in the first place and no "approve this login" prompt an attacker can social-engineer a user into accepting for a session they didn't initiate — the cryptographic challenge is bound to the specific legitimate site, full stop.

Despite that, the same 2026 survey data found 57% of organizations still rely on phishable authentication (passwords, in most cases, even where some MFA exists) for primary sign-in — meaning the gap between what's available and what's actually deployed remains wide, even now.

## A realistic rollout plan

Passkeys don't need to replace every credential in your organization on day one. A staged rollout that most businesses can realistically execute:

1. **Start with high-value targets**: admin accounts, email, and any system tied to financial approvals — exactly the accounts BEC attacks target most.
2. **Enable passkeys alongside existing passwords**, not as a forced replacement, during the transition — most major identity providers and SaaS platforms support this dual-mode setup today.
3. **Plan device loss and account recovery before rollout**, not after the first lockout — document how an employee regains access if their passkey-holding device is lost or replaced.
4. **Communicate the "why," briefly**, to employees — framing it as faster and harder to phish (both true) drives adoption better than framing it as a compliance mandate.
5. **Track adoption, not just availability.** Enabling passkeys as an option and having employees actually use them regularly are different numbers, per the FIDO data above — plan a follow-up push once initial rollout completes.

## Passkey rollout checklist

- [ ] Confirm which of your core systems (email, admin panels, financial tools) support passkey authentication
- [ ] Start rollout with the highest-value accounts first (admin, email, financial approvals)
- [ ] Enable passkeys alongside passwords during transition, not as a forced cutover
- [ ] Document account recovery procedure for lost/replaced devices before rollout, not after
- [ ] Communicate rollout to employees with a clear, brief "why" (faster, phishing-resistant)
- [ ] Track actual regular usage post-rollout, not just how many accounts have passkeys enabled
- [ ] Revisit remaining password-only accounts periodically and migrate as support becomes available

## Key takeaways

Passkey adoption crossed a real tipping point in 2026 — billions in active use, majority enterprise deployment or piloting, and measurably better security and usability than passwords at the same time, which is unusual for a security control. The gap that remains isn't technology readiness; it's that most organizations haven't yet rolled passkeys out to the accounts that matter most, with 57% still relying on phishable credentials for primary sign-in even now. Starting with your highest-value accounts — admin, email, financial systems — closes the exact gap that modern BEC attacks exploit.
