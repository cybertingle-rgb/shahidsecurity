In April 2026, a coordinated international action led by the US Department of Justice's Scam Center Strike Force seized 503 fake investment websites and a Telegram recruitment channel with over 6,000 followers used to funnel forced labor into a Cambodian scam compound. By February 2026, the Strike Force reported that crypto seizures tied to these operations had topped $580 million. The action produced 276 arrests internationally. This is what "pig butchering" — the industry's own grim term for the slow, patient fattening-up of a victim before the financial slaughter — looks like at industrial scale in 2026.

## How the scam actually works

The pattern is consistent across reported cases, and understanding it is the single most useful thing a business can teach employees:

1. **Contact.** A text message that appears to be a wrong number, a match on a dating app, or a professional-sounding connection on a platform like LinkedIn.
2. **Rapport.** Weeks, sometimes months, of patient, genuine-feeling conversation — building trust with no ask for money. This is deliberate; it's what distinguishes pig butchering from a rushed scam.
3. **The pitch.** An eventual, casual mention of a cryptocurrency investment opportunity the scammer claims personal success with, often pointing to a professional-looking trading platform.
4. **The hook.** The target is encouraged to invest a small amount first, sees apparent gains on the platform, and may even be allowed to withdraw a small sum successfully — building real confidence before the trap closes.
5. **The slaughter.** Once a larger sum is deposited, withdrawals are blocked, the "investment opportunity" collapses, or the scammer vanishes along with the funds.

## The organized, industrial scale behind it

What makes 2026's enforcement actions notable isn't just the dollar figures — it's the scale of the infrastructure. Compounds operating out of Myanmar, Cambodia, and Laos are reported to hold tens of thousands of people, many trafficked and forced to run these scam operations under threat, working from scripted conversation playbooks across thousands of simultaneous targets. US authorities have pursued charges against individuals tied to specific compounds and have separately reported a $15 billion Bitcoin seizure connected to Cambodia's Prince Group. This is not a story about isolated bad actors — it's an industrial fraud economy with its own labor force, infrastructure, and laundering networks.

## What this means for your business

**Employee awareness.** Executives and employees with any public professional presence (LinkedIn especially) are realistic targets — the "professional connection that turns into investment advice" pattern specifically exploits workplace-adjacent trust. This belongs in the same security-awareness training as phishing and vishing: see our guide to [helpdesk vishing and social engineering](/blog/helpdesk-vishing-social-engineering-2026/) for the related pattern of attackers exploiting trust and patience rather than urgency.

**Financial exposure.** If your business accepts or processes crypto payments, understand that laundering proceeds from these operations flow through legitimate-looking exchanges and payment rails — the same counterparty-screening discipline covered in our piece on [sanctioned crypto exchanges](/blog/sanctioned-crypto-exchanges-business-counterparty-risk/) is directly relevant here, since laundered pig-butchering proceeds are a documented use case for the kind of exchange infrastructure under increasing sanctions scrutiny.

**Reporting.** If an employee reports being targeted — even if no money was lost — treat it as a genuine security incident worth documenting, both to protect that employee and because a successful approach against one employee often means others were targeted too.

## The practical takeaway

The DOJ's Scam Center Strike Force has said it remains "committed" to continued action against the transnational networks behind these compounds, and Elliptic and other analytics firms expect enforcement to keep intensifying through the rest of 2026. But enforcement against the compounds doesn't reduce the exposure of your own employees and systems in the meantime — awareness is still the actual control. A [security awareness session](/services/monitoring-training/) that covers this alongside phishing and vishing is a practical, low-cost way to close that gap before it becomes your business's problem.
